About Me

I am an independent researcher in machine learning. I hold a master's degree in Informatics from Kyoto University.

My research focuses on generative modeling, particularly flow and diffusion models. I am interested in how training objectives and sampling methods affect generation quality and computational cost.

I am currently seeking research assistant (RA) positions and Ph.D. opportunities in machine learning. I welcome remote research opportunities and am also open to on-site positions.

Research Interests

Generative Modeling and Distribution Transport

Flow and diffusion models, stochastic bridges, and the relationship between local dynamics and generated distributions.

Learning Dynamics and Supervision

How training objectives, label noise, and model structure shape learning, and when better fitting leads to better task performance.

Efficient Generation and Inference

Few-step sampling, distillation, and the trade-offs between generation quality, training cost, and inference time.

Current Research

Finite-Step Generation, Learning, and Transport

Ongoing research

I investigate the relationship between finite-time prediction, distribution transport, and the behavior of trained generators. This work examines how target bias and fitting errors affect a sequence of generation steps, and evaluates supervision for adapting pretrained models to few-step generation.

Stochastic Bridges and Structured Generative Learning

Ongoing research

Building on my master's research, I study generative models based on stochastic bridges. I examine how path design, supervision, and model structure affect learning and sampling, with the aim of using probabilistic structure to improve generation at a finite number of model evaluations.
